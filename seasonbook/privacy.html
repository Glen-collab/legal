Season Book

Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 8, 2026
The short version: Season Book has no accounts and no servers. Your gymnast's scores and photographs stay on your phone, and in your own iCloud (iPhone) or your own Google Drive (Android) if you use the off-device copy — neither of which we can see into. The app never reads your photo library on either platform. Location data is stripped from every photograph it stores. We collect nothing, we track nothing, and there are no ads.

Information we collect

None. Season Book has no sign-up, no login, no account, and no server of ours for your information to be sent to. We do not collect your name, email address, location, contacts, or device identifiers. There is no analytics package, no advertising, no crash reporting, and no third-party tracking code in the app.

Where your content is stored

Gymnasts, meets, scores, teams and photographs are stored on your own phone. What happens beyond that differs by platform, and the difference is worth reading.

On iPhone

Your content syncs through your own iCloud account so it appears on your other Apple devices. This uses Apple's CloudKit private database. It is your iCloud account, it counts against your iCloud storage, and we have no access to it. Apple's handling of that data is covered by Apple's Privacy Policy.

If iCloud is unavailable — you are signed out, or out of space — the app keeps working and stores everything locally instead. There is no setting to turn sync off; signing out of iCloud, or turning this app off under iCloud in iOS Settings, has that effect.

On Android

The Android version has no sync. It offers an optional backup to your own Google Drive, and it is off until you turn it on.

When you turn it on, Season Book asks for a single Google permission: drive.appdata. This is the narrowest access Google Drive offers. It allows an app to read and write only files it created itself, inside a hidden folder that does not appear among your Drive files and that no other app can open. Season Book cannot see, list, or touch anything else in your Drive — not your documents, not your photos, not files shared with you. We are not asking for access to your Drive; we are asking for a private cupboard inside it.

The backup is one file holding her scores and photographs. It is stored in your own Google account, counts against your own Google storage, and we have no access to it. Google's handling of that data is covered by Google's Privacy Policy. With the backup switched off, everything stays on the phone and nothing leaves it.

Android can also import a backup exported from the iPhone app. You choose the file; the app reads it and adds what is in it. Nothing is uploaded and nothing is sent to us.

Photographs of children

This app exists to keep pictures of somebody's child next to her scores, so this section is the one that matters.

  • The photographs never leave your devices and your iCloud account. They are not uploaded to us. There is no server of ours for them to be uploaded to. No one but you can see them unless you choose to send them to somebody.
  • Location data is removed from every photograph the app stores. A photograph taken on a phone usually carries GPS coordinates, a timestamp, and the camera make and model. Season Book re-encodes each image and that metadata does not survive — not in the stored copy, not in the thumbnail, and not in the copies embedded in a season book you share. This is deliberate: a photograph of a child should not tell a stranger which gym she trains at.
  • Nothing analyses the photographs. No face detection, no recognition, no tagging, no machine learning of any kind. They are pictures in a list.
  • No video. Deliberately. Video is heavier, harder to strip cleanly, and a bigger thing to hand around than a photograph.

Camera and photo library

On Android, Season Book asks for no permissions at all. It holds no camera permission and no access to your gallery. Taking a picture hands the job to whichever camera app you already use; choosing an existing one goes through Android's own photo picker. Both hand back only the images you picked. The only things the Android app declares are internet access and the ability to see whether there is a connection, used by the Google Drive backup and by casting to a television.

On iPhone, Season Book asks for two permissions, and only when you tap the control that needs one:

  • Camera — to photograph your gymnast at a meet.
  • Add to photo library — used only by "save this meet's photos to an album", so you can run Apple's own slideshow and put it on a television.

The app cannot read your photo library, on either platform. That is not a promise about our intentions; it is what the app is capable of. Adding photographs you have already taken uses the operating system's own photo picker — Apple's on iPhone, Android's on Android — which runs outside the app and hands over only the images you pick. The app is never granted access to the library itself, and it does not ask for it.

Other people's children

If you bring a team into a meet, or photograph a group, you are handling other people's children — their names, their scores, and their faces.

The app treats them exactly like your own: stored on your device, and in your own iCloud or Google Drive if you use that, never sent to us, never published. But two things are yours to manage rather than ours:

  • A season book is a shareable file, and it carries whatever photographs and names are in it. Before sending one to anybody outside your own family, consider who else appears in it. Other parents may reasonably have views about where a picture of their child ends up.
  • If another parent asks you to remove their child, swipe to delete that gymnast and her scores and photographs go with her.

Sharing a season book, and exporting

Season Book can turn a season, or a whole career, into a PDF; it can send a single meet as a picture; and it can export everything as an archive of spreadsheets, web pages and plain photographs. All of these are created on your own phone and go wherever you send them using the standard share sheet — iOS's on iPhone, Android's on Android. Nothing is uploaded, published, or hosted by us, and no link to your content exists on the internet. Once you send a file to someone, what happens to it is between you and them.

Network use

Season Book has no server of ours to talk to and works fully offline on both platforms.

On iPhone, the only network traffic is Apple's own iCloud sync, between your device and your iCloud account.

On Android, there are two, and only if you use them: the Google Drive backup, between your phone and your own Google account; and casting to a television.

Casting deserves a plain description, because it is the one place photographs travel. When you put a season on a Chromecast, the photographs go from your phone to the television over your own home network — sent directly along the Cast connection, the same way the scores are. They are not uploaded anywhere, they are not stored on a server, and they are never given a web address. The page the television displays is a fixed page of ours that holds no data of its own and fetches nothing; everything on screen arrives from the phone in the room. This is deliberate: a page that could fetch your daughter's photographs would be a page anybody could fetch them from.

Deleting your data

There is no account to delete, because there is no account. Everything is removable from inside the app by swiping:

  • A gymnast — her scores and photographs go with her.
  • A meet, a team, or a single photograph.

Android also has Settings → Delete All My Data, which removes every gymnast, meet, score and photograph from the device in one step. Export first if you want to keep a copy.

iPhone: deletions propagate to your other devices and to your iCloud account. "Export Everything" will give you a complete archive first if you want one. Deleting the app removes the on-device copy; you can remove the remaining iCloud copy in iOS Settings under your name → iCloud → Manage Account Storage.

Android: if the backup is switched on, the copy in your Google Drive is replaced by an empty one at the next backup, and the app says so on the delete button before you press it. If you want that copy gone immediately rather than at the next backup, use Settings → Stop backing up and choose Stop and Delete Backup. You can also revoke Season Book's access entirely at myaccount.google.com/permissions, or delete the hidden data from drive.google.com → Settings → Manage apps. Deleting the app from your phone removes the on-device copy but leaves whatever is in Drive.

Children using the app

Season Book is a record-keeping tool for a parent or coach, not an app for children to use, and it is not in the App Store's Kids Category. It does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children — because it does not collect information from anyone at all.
